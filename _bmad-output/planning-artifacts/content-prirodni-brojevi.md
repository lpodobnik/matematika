## Units

### 1. Natural numbers, zero, even and odd (pp. 88–91)

**He can:** say what N and N₀ contain, write `10 ∈ N` and `0 ∉ N`, and tell even from odd at a glance, for any size of number.

**The idea:** natural numbers are what you get by counting, so counting starts at 1 and never ends. Zero means there is nothing to count, so it joins only in N₀. A number is even if things pair up with none left over: 15 is 7 pairs and 1 left.

**Why the last digit decides (added):** every ten, hundred and thousand pairs up exactly, so only the ones can leave one over.

**Words:** prirodni broj, skup, N (čitaj: en), N₀ (čitaj: en nula), znamenka, parni, neparni, ∈, ∉.

**Tasks:** read a tally board; sort numbers into even and odd; build the largest or smallest number from given digits; list all safe codes that fit clues (`2 5 2 _`, even).

**Traps:** 0 is not natural. 1 is the smallest natural number, and there is no largest.

**Side note:** the book contrasts Egyptian numerals, where a symbol is worth the same anywhere, with ours, where a digit's value depends on its place. This sets up unit 2.

### 2. Big numbers and powers of ten (pp. 92–95)

**He can:** read and write numbers past a million, in digits and in words; write 1 000 000 as 10⁶; write 354 as `3 · 10² + 5 · 10 + 4`; and judge which such sum is largest without calculating.

**The idea:** moving a digit one place left makes it worth 10 times more. A power of ten is a short way to write tens multiplied together: 10⁶ is six tens multiplied, read "deset na šestu". The small number is also how many zeros follow the 1. The book builds it from a library: 10 shelves, 10 books, 10 pages, 10 lines, 10 words, 10 letters gives 10⁶ letters.

**Why the zeros match (added):** multiplying by 10 adds one zero, so six tens add six zeros.

**Words:** potencija, dekadska jedinica, brojevni izraz. Number names, in steps of a thousand:

| 10³ | 10⁶ | 10⁹ | 10¹² | 10¹⁵ | 10¹⁸ | 10²¹ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tisuća | milijun | **milijarda** | **bilijun** | bilijarda | trilijun | trilijarda |

Croatian uses the long scale. 10⁹ is *milijarda*, and *bilijun* is 10¹², so English "billion" must never be translated as *bilijun*.

**Do not use** the words *baza* and *eksponent*. The curriculum says not to introduce them in 5th grade.

**Tasks:** read aloud up to 17 digits; words to digits; the number one more or one less; metric units as powers (`1 km = 10³ m`, `1 km = 10⁵ cm`); match sizes to real things (population, distance to the Sun).

**Traps:**
- Zeros inside numbers, in words and in digits: *trideset tisuća petnaest* is 30 015.
- One more across nines (599 999 → 600 000) and one less across zeros (506 000 → 505 999).
- `0 · 10³` is easy to drop when a place holds zero.

### 3. The number line (pp. 96–98)

**He can:** read a number at a point, place a number on a line, work out the step from two labelled points, and choose a sensible unit for the numbers he has to show.

**The idea:** a number line is a line with a chosen **jedinična dužina**, repeated at equal steps. Equal steps mean a difference is always the same distance. The step does not have to be 1, and the line does not have to show zero: 997 to 1 004 or 103 000 to 103 011 are just a part of it.

**Words:** brojevni pravac, jedinična dužina. The curriculum says not to introduce *ishodište* and *jedinična točka*, although the book marks those points as O and E.

**Tasks:** read circled points (labels every 10, ticks every 1); fill boxes between 16 and 21; place long-jump results between 230 and 240; place birth years 1850–1900; draw 80 to 230, choosing his own unit.

**Traps:** counting ticks instead of working out what one tick is worth.

The number line also carries units 7 and 12: adding is jumping (9 then 5 lands on 14), and the mean is drawn on it.

### 4. Neighbours: predecessor and successor (pp. 99–101)

**He can:** give both neighbours of any number and write them as `n − 1` and `n + 1`.

**The idea:** the neighbours are one less and one more. Every natural number has a successor. Every natural number except 1 has a predecessor inside N, because 0 is not natural.

**Words:** neposredni prethodnik, neposredni sljedbenik.

**Tasks:** neighbours of 20 000, 899, 1 000, 100 000 000; neighbour of a calculated result; a crossword of number words.

**Traps:**
- Crossing a boundary: 20 000 → 19 999, 899 → 900.
- 1 has no predecessor in N, but in N₀ it is 0, and 0 has none.
- Nested wording: the predecessor of the number whose predecessor is 17 is 17.

### 5. Comparing and ranges (pp. 102–106)

**He can:** compare numbers of any size; order long lists; read and write all six signs; list what a range such as `2 ≤ x < 9` contains; and read it in set notation.

**The idea:** more digits means larger. With the same number of digits, the first place from the left where they differ decides.

**Why the left wins (added):** one unit of a higher place outweighs everything to its right, as 1 000 beats 999.

A range is two comparisons at once: `<` leaves the end number out, and `≤` keeps it in.

**Words:** je manji, je veći, je jednak, je različit (≠), je manji ili jednak (≤), je veći ili jednak (≥), produžena nejednakost.

**Tasks:**
- List `2 ≤ x < 9` (2 to 8, seven numbers) and `2 < x ≤ 9` (3 to 9).
- For `A = {x: x ∈ N i 3 ≤ x < 30}`, is `3 ∈ A`? Is `30 ∈ A`?
- Which numbers fit `12 + ♥ < 20` in N₀ (0 to 7)?
- Rank real tables: mountains, planets, 22 counties.
- Order animals from clues: elephant heavier than rhino, crocodile lighter than rhino, crocodile heavier than giraffe.

**Traps:**
- Same digits, different order: 5 202 and 5 022, 8 576 123 and 8 567 321.
- More digits beats bigger-looking digits: 800 309 > 98 009.
- More things is not always more value: 3 bottles at 1 € against 2 bottles at 2 €.
- Whether the end number counts, and whether 0 counts.

### 6. Rounding and estimating (pp. 107–109)

**He can:** round to any place from tens to millions, write `≈`, work out the error, go backwards from a rounded number, and estimate before calculating.

**The idea:** rounding picks the nearest round number, because often an exact number doesn't matter. Only the digit just right of the place decides: 0–4 goes down, 5–9 goes up. The number line shows it: 41–44 go to 40, 45–49 go to 50. 45 is exactly halfway, and the rule sends it up. The error is the distance between the true and the rounded number: 121 ≈ 100, error 21.

**Words:** zaokruživanje, približno (≈), pogreška pri zaokruživanju, desetica, stotica, tisućica.

**Tasks:**
- Round prices to tens and give the error.
- Round the same number to tens, hundreds and thousands.
- Backwards: a book has "about 330" pages, so 325 to 334. Fish rounded to hundreds are 4 200, so 4 150 to 4 249.
- Estimate a bill, then calculate and compare: 475 + 699 + 139 + 299 + 159 = 1 771, about 2 000.
- Which jumpers jumped "about 150 cm"? 146, 148 and 152 do, and 155 does not.

**Traps:**
- Carrying: 99 → 100, 199 → 200, 7 999 → 8 000.
- Rounding in steps is wrong: 18 499 000 to millions is 18 000 000, not 19 000 000. Only the next digit counts.

### 7. Adding and subtracting (pp. 110–120)

**He can:** add and subtract in columns up to 9 digits, regroup a sum to make it easy, and turn "za koliko" wording into the right operation.

**The idea:**
- Adding is shortened counting.
- Subtracting finds the other addend, when you know the sum and one addend, so it can always be checked by adding.
- Order and grouping don't matter when adding, so look for pairs that make round numbers: Gauss added 1 to 100 as 50 pairs of 101, which is 5 050.
- Subtracting two things in a row is subtracting their sum: `423 − 37 − 54 = 423 − (37 + 54)`.

**Why columns (added):** only like units add, ones with ones and tens with tens. Ten of one place become one of the next.

**Words:** pribrojnici, zbroj (suma); umanjenik, umanjitelj, razlika (diferencija); komutativnost (zamjena), `a + b = b + a`; asocijativnost (združivanje), `(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)`. Zero changes nothing: `n + 0 = n`, `a − 0 = a`, `a − a = 0`.

**Tasks:**
- `637 + 655 + 363`, adding 637 and 363 first.
- Eight six-digit numbers added "in eight seconds", because they pair into round totals.
- From six numbers, make three sums with round results.
- Fill a table with row and column totals, where the corner cell checks both.
- A lift carries at most 350 kg: who can ride so the load is over 300 kg? There are several answers.
- `358 − 179 − 158 + 179`, where 179 cancels.

**Predicting:** if one addend grows by 47 and the other by 26, the sum grows by 73, whatever the numbers were.

**Traps:**
- In N₀ the umanjenik can't be smaller than the umanjitelj.
- Borrowing across zeros: `300 000 000 − 189 368`.
- With only + and −, go left to right.
- Smallest number from given digits can't start with 0.

### 8. Multiplying (pp. 121–127)

**He can:** multiply by 10, 100 and 1 000 at a glance, multiply round numbers in his head, multiply in columns (the book goes as far as `78 965 · 2 013`), and estimate first.

**The idea:** multiplying is shortened adding of equal addends. 6 sacks of 12 kg is a grid of 6 rows of 12.

**Why the rows shift (added):** in `253 · 48`, the 4 means 40, so `253 · 48 = 253 · 40 + 253 · 8`. Written multiplication is unit 9's distributivity.

**Words:** faktori, umnožak (produkt), `n · 1 = n`, `n · 0 = 0`. Several factors go left to right.

**Tasks:** `937 · 40`, `535 · 700`; `934 · 308` (zero in the middle); units by multiplying (`89 km = _ m`); "koji je broj 54 puta veći"; best estimate for `28 · 3`; a shopping basket as a sum of products; find three true products from two lists.

**Traps:**
- A hidden zero makes everything 0: `37 · 56 · 94 · 0 · 192`.
- Facts the task doesn't give: a horse has 4 hooves; April has 30 days; a leap year has 366.
- *54 puta veći* multiplies, *za 54 veći* adds.

**A nice discovery in the book:** `848 · 129 = 424 · 258`. Halve one factor, double the other, and the product stays.

### 9. Properties of multiplication, distributivity (pp. 128–133)

**He can:** rearrange a product to make it easy, name the property he used, and spot and take out a common factor.

**The idea, all from counting the same things two ways:**
- **Komutativnost:** a grid of trees counted by rows or by columns: `3 · 7 = 7 · 3`.
- **Asocijativnost:** coins in piggy banks grouped differently: `3 · (2 · 5) = (3 · 2) · 5`.
- **Distributivnost:** grandpa plants 3 rows of 5 and grandma 4 rows of 5. Count all the rows, or count each person's: `5 · (3 + 4) = 5 · 3 + 5 · 4`. Also for subtraction, with `a ≥ b`.
- **Izlučivanje zajedničkog faktora** is the same equality read backwards: `7 · 450 + 13 · 450 = 450 · (7 + 13) = 9 000`.

**Words:** the four names above, and raspodjela (distribution).

**The pairs he should know (the book's šalabahter):** `2 · 5 = 10`, `20 · 5 = 100`, `25 · 4 = 100`, `125 · 8 = 1 000`.

**Tasks:**
- `25 · 73 · 4`, `125 · 7 · 8`.
- `409 · 86 − 309 · 86 = 100 · 86` (sample test).
- `26 · 13 + 13 · 74`, where the factor changes side.
- `37 · 99 = 37 · 100 − 37`; `147 · 11`; `563 · 101`.
- Word problems with a common factor: laps of 52 m, 8 laps then 12.

**Traps:**
- `209 · 99 + 209`: the last 209 is `209 · 1`, so it is `209 · 100`.
- `200 · 400 − 400 · 200` is 0.
- Three terms: `105 · 37 + 105 · 28 − 15 · 105 = 105 · 50`.
- Applying the rule only to the first term in the bracket (not named in the book; the usual mistake).

### 10. Dividing, with remainder (pp. 134–140)

**He can:** divide in columns (as far as `12 622 002 : 6` and `753 200 : 56`), divide by round numbers, find the nepotpuni količnik and ostatak, check by multiplying, and say what a remainder means in a story.

**The idea:** division finds the missing factor: `20 · □ = 120`. It answers two different questions:
- **How many groups?** 120 sacks, 20 on each cart: `120 : 20 = 6` carts.
- **How many in each?** 120 sacks on 6 carts: `120 : 6 = 20` sacks.

**The book blurs these.** On p. 134 it asks how many carts, then writes `120 : 6 = 20`. The game must keep them clear.

**Rules, all explained by multiplication (the book does this):**
- `n : n = 1`, `n : 1 = n`, `0 : n = 0`, because `0 · n = 0`.
- **S nulom se ne dijeli:** `7 : 0` has no answer, because no number times 0 gives 7.
- `djelitelj · nepotpuni količnik + ostatak = djeljenik`: `6 · 5 + 2 = 32`.
- The remainder is always smaller than the divisor.

**Why (added):** if the remainder reached the divisor, you could make one more group.

**Words:** djeljenik, djelitelj, količnik (kvocijent), nepotpuni količnik, ostatak, dijeljenje s ostatkom; signs `:` and `/`.

**Tasks:**
- Spot the only correct one: `71 : 9 = 9 ost. 0`, `257 : 4 = 64 ost. 1`, `55 : 9 = 5 ost. 10`.
- Without calculating: is `924 : 4` nearer 200 or 300? Which is smaller, `472 : 8` or `472 : 59`?
- Possible remainders when dividing by 5 (0 to 4).
- Which number divided by 87 gives 356, remainder 2?
- Roses in bunches of 6: 5 847 makes 974 bunches and 3 left.
- Side of a square from its perimeter; instalments; units by dividing (`5 700 dm² = 57 m²`).

**Traps:**
- Dividing by 0, and thinking `0 : 3` is also forbidden.
- A remainder as large as the divisor.
- Several divisions go left to right: `24 : 2 : 3`, and `3 000 : 24 : 5`.
- *3 puta manje* divides; *za 3 manje* subtracts.

### 11. Order of operations and brackets (pp. 141–147)

**He can:** calculate any expression with the four operations and nested brackets, showing one step per line; place brackets to make an equality true; and turn a story into one expression.

**The rules:**
1. Inside brackets first, from the innermost out: ( ), then [ ], then { }.
2. Then multiplication and division (*viši stupanj*), left to right.
3. Then addition and subtraction (*niži stupanj*), left to right.
4. Copy everything not yet calculated, and keep a bracket until it holds one number.

**Why multiply first (added):** `2 + 3 · 4` means 2 and three fours. Multiplication is a shortened addition, so that group has to be finished before anything joins it. Brackets say "treat this as one number". The rule also means everyone gets the same answer. The book gives only the rules and a cartoon of `2 + 2 : 2`, where one boy says 2 and the other, correctly, says 3.

**Words:** brojevni izraz, računske radnje višeg i nižeg stupnja, okrugle, uglate, vitičaste zagrade, brojevno stablo.

**Tasks:**
- `100 − 20 · 3 + 10 : 2 = 45`.
- `450 − (326 − (70 + 2 · 3)) = 200`.
- Find the only correct one: `28 − 4 · 5 = 120`, `16 + 20 : 4 = 9`, `36 − 4 · 8 = 4`, `5 + 2 · 3 = 2`.
- Place brackets: `3 + 4 · 3 + 3 = 24` becomes `(3 + 4) · 3 + 3`.
- The same numbers with different brackets, all 5s: `5 + 5 · (5 − 5) − 5`.
- Number trees: read the tree as an expression, then calculate.
- Story to expression: 14 boys and 13 girls, 6 notebooks each: `(14 + 13) · 6`.
- Working backwards from a total: the day's takings 1 715, minus what is known, leaves 400 for oil at 8 a bottle: 50 bottles.

**Traps:**
- Going left to right regardless of rank, which is exactly how the wrong answers above were made.
- A minus before a bracket: `258 − 34 + 41` is 265, `258 − (34 + 41)` is 183.
- Division and multiplication in a row: `8 : 2 · 5` is 20, not `8 : 10`.

### 12. Arithmetic mean (pp. 148–149)

**He can:** calculate a mean, say what it tells him, and find numbers that have a given mean.

**The idea:** the mean is the sum of all the data divided by how many there are. The book draws grades as stacks of squares on a number line, and the mean is where a seesaw (*klackalica*) balances: `(4·5 + 3·4 + 6·3 + 3·2 + 4·1) : 20 = 3`.

**Why it works (added):** level the stacks by moving from tall to short. The total doesn't change and everyone ends up equal, so each gets total : count.

The mean need not be a value anyone really has: the book jokes about an average family with one and a half cars.

**Words:** aritmetička sredina, prosječna (srednja) vrijednost, `x̄`.

**Tasks:** mean of grades, masses, temperatures, daily takings (`21 000 : 6 = 3 500`); average daily spending for September (`1 140 : 30 = 38`, sample test); two boys with mean mass 72, each between 60 and 85: find as many pairs as possible.

**Traps:** forgetting a repeated value in the count (four fives count four times); dividing by the wrong count.

Officially this is extended content (*prošireni sadržaj*), but the book teaches it and tests it.

### 13. Reading data (pp. 150–157)

**He can:** read a table and a chart, turn one into the other, add and compare what he reads, and answer questions that go beyond reading.

**The idea:** a chart is a table you can see at a glance. First check what one step on the axis is worth.

**Words:** tablica, stupac, redak, stupčasti dijagram, točkasti dijagram, linijski dijagram.

**Chart types in the book:** bars, dots, a double bar chart (roses and tulips), stacked cells (girls and boys by height group), and blocks where one block means 10 pupils.

**Tasks:** read grades from bars and total the class; who won a class vote; which days sold more roses than tulips; at what hour the classroom was warmest; how much less one bar is than another; why fewer babies are born at weekends.

**Traps:**
- The axis step isn't 1, so bars fall between the gridlines.
- One block means 10.
- "Veću od" and "više od" leave out the boundary; "od 2013. do 2016." includes both ends.
- Height groups can only give a group as the most common answer, not one height.

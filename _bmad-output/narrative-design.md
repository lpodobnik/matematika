## Story Foundation

### Narrative Premise

A villain has kidnapped the Clockmaker, and the city has seized up because nobody understands the written steps left behind. An apprentice nobody takes seriously finds a notebook and is the first to work out why the machines run. If he fails, the automatons strip the city for parts until nothing is left to restart.

### Core Themes

1. **Knowing why beats knowing the steps.** That's why the city stopped, and why the villain took the Clockmaker. The story never says this outright.
2. **The kid can fix what the adults can't.** This carries the core fantasy's "when nobody thought I could".
3. **What you make is yours.** The city's log records his restarts, and his rooms hang in the workshop.

### Tone and Atmosphere

**Tone:** Wry adventure. Townspeople are dry-humoured, the stakes are real, and the villain's marks are threatening but a little absurd.

**Atmosphere:** A grey blueprint city that gains brass, motion and sound as he restarts it.

**Emotional Register:** He goes from being doubted to being the one they ask, with pride in what he's restarted and curiosity about who took the Clockmaker.

**Kept out:** nothing from Luka and his son's table game appears in the city.

---

## Story Structure

### Structure Type

**Episodic, with one overarching arc: find the Clockmaker.**

Each district is an episode built around one new tool, and it ends on a clue. Tools arrive one at a time and the number of districts is open, so no episode can depend on a fixed ending.

### Act Breakdown

| Part of district 1 | Rooms | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 1–3 | He finds the notebook and restarts the first machines while townspeople doubt him. |
| Middle | 4–8 | The district wakes and people start asking for him. The old logbook (room 7) shows the Clockmaker didn't leave but was taken. |
| End | 9–13 | The villain's marks get bolder. The clock tower gives a clue pointing to the foundry gate, which stays shut. |

### The Logbook Twist

The logbook has `3x = 18`, then the wrong line `x = 15`, which treats `3x` as 3 + x. The Clockmaker never makes that mistake, so it must be deliberate: forced to write under the villain's eye, the Clockmaker planted an error only someone who understands would notice. The villain missed it, which shows the villain knows the steps but not why they work.

When he later plants a mistake for a friend, he's using the Clockmaker's own trick.

## Pacing and Flow

### Narrative Tempo

Story arrives only at restarts, one or two lines at a time, so it never interrupts a room.

### Tension Curve

Tension rises in waves, peaking at room 7 and again at room 13.

### Story Density

Rooms 6 and 10 are breathers. Rooms 5–6 and 9–10 can come in either order, so their lines can't refer to each other.

### Key Moments

**Highest tension:** room 7, when he learns the Clockmaker was taken.
**Emotional climax:** room 13, with every machine sounding together.
**Resolution beat:** the foundry gate stays shut, which leads into the next district.

## Characters

Names follow real inventors and mathematicians, as set under World Building. The villain's backstory and ending belong to later districts.

### Protagonist

#### The apprentice

**Description:** Named and picked by the player; the name stays on the device. Silent, like Link. Others talk to him, which suits short lines and avoids gendered forms.

**Background:** An apprentice nobody takes seriously, who finds a notebook after the city stops.

**Motivation:** Get the city moving, and work out why the machines run.

**Strengths:** He pokes things nobody else will.

**Flaws:** None written in. He has whatever doubts the player brings.

**Conflicts:**

- Internal: whether he may do things that aren't in the book.
- External: townspeople's doubt, the automatons, and the First Automaton's undoing of his repairs.

### Antagonist

#### The First Automaton

**Description:** Majstor Kristijan's first machine, and the only other character asking why. It speaks in painfully literal punched-tape notices, for example "THIS BRIDGE WAS WORKING. THIS HAS BEEN CORRECTED." and "YOU ARE NOT IN THE STEPS. PLEASE STOP." Final text is Croatian.

**Background:** Built to run the city by the written steps. When the steps stopped being enough, it took its maker to ask why.

**Goals:** To be told why the machines work.

**Methods:** Commands the other automatons, strips the city for parts, corrects repairs that aren't in the steps, and makes Kristijan keep writing under its eye. It reads his lines without understanding them, which is why it misses the planted mistake.

**Relationship to Protagonist:** He is an anomaly to it, something the steps don't cover. They don't meet in the MVP.

**Sympathetic Elements:** It's asking his question the wrong way, so it can be funny, scary and a little sad.

### Supporting Characters

#### Majstor Kristijan (the Clockmaker)

**Role:** Absent mentor, named after Christiaan Huygens, who invented the pendulum clock.
**Personality:** Exact and sly. He never makes a careless mistake, so a mistake from him means something.
**Function:** Speaks only through notebooks, the logbook's planted mistake and the drum trace.
**Key Moments:** Room 7, the planted mistake. Room 13, the last drum trace.

#### The conductor

**Role:** Doubter and comic relief.
**Personality:** Clutches the book. "That's not in the book."
**Function:** Gives the doubt in "when nobody thought I could" a face.
**Key Moments:** Start and room 1, the doubt. Room 10, settling up at the market.

#### The lamplighter's kid

**Role:** First fan.
**Personality:** Curious and impressed, and asks "how did you do that?"
**Function:** Explanation cards come as answers to this question rather than from a teacher.
**Key Moments:** Room 2, the first question. Later rooms, copying him, badly.

#### The old mechanic

**Role:** Shopkeeper and source of backstory.
**Personality:** Unhurried, knows more about Kristijan than they first say.
**Function:** Sells upgrades for parts and hands over the workshop.
**Key Moments:** Room 6, handing over the workshop.

## World Building

### World Overview

**Setting:** Satograd (placeholder; "sat" means clock, "grad" means city), a clockwork city. The MVP covers its first district, Donji Kotač.

**World Type:** Clockwork and steam, with no magic and no electricity. Lamps burn oil.

**World Rules:**
- Machines obey the maths exactly, so anything can be worked out. That keeps the puzzles fair, and it's why poking at things works.
- Everything is a mechanism, so balancing, fitting and splitting are natural.

**Atmosphere:** A grey blueprint city that gains brass, motion and sound as he restarts it.

**Unique Elements:** People and places are named after real inventors and mathematicians, including Croatian ones he may later meet at school. The names go unexplained, as things he can discover later.

---

### History and Backstory

**Timeline Overview:**
1. Majstor Kristijan built the city's machines, wrote their settings in notebooks and built the First Automaton to run them.
2. People came to rely on the written steps, and nobody needed to know why they worked.
3. As things wore and changed, the steps stopped being enough, so the First Automaton took Kristijan to ask why.
4. The city seized up.

**Major Events:** The kidnapping, which happened before the game starts and is revealed in room 7.

**Legends and Myths:** The Book. People still turn the handles three times every morning because it says so. That's the opening joke.

**Hidden Secrets:** Kristijan is still signalling, and only someone who understands can read it.

---

### Factions and Organizations

#### The townspeople

**Purpose:** Keep the city going by the book.
**Leadership:** None. The Book is the authority.
**Goals:** Get things working again without having to understand them.
**Methods:** Repeat the written steps.
**Relationships:** Fear the automatons; doubt the apprentice at first.
**Story Role:** Their shift from doubt to asking for him is his arc made visible.

#### The automatons

**Purpose:** Kristijan's helpers, now carrying out the First Automaton's orders.
**Leadership:** The First Automaton.
**Goals:** Gather parts and correct anything not in the steps.
**Methods:** Strip the city for parts, seal machines, leave punched-tape notices.
**Relationships:** Opposed to anything off the steps, the apprentice most of all.
**Story Role:** Fights, and the villain's presence without the villain.

The apprentice belongs to neither faction.

## Dialogue Framework

### Dialogue Style

**Overall Voice:** Wry, short, everyday Croatian.

**Style Elements:**

- Formality: casual
- Period: timeless, readable today
- Verbosity: one or two lines per moment, each no longer than about 80 characters, so they fit a tablet speech bubble
- Humor: dry
- Profanity: none, and no slang

**Grammar rule:** nobody addresses him with a gendered past tense. Lines use the present tense, commands, or talk about the machine ("Kako to radi?", not "Kako si to napravio?").

**Character Voice Distinctions:**

| Character | How they speak |
|---|---|
| Gospon Penkala | Quotes the Book ("Prema Knjizi…"), with a light Zagreb kajkavian flavour ("kaj", "gospon"). Only he uses dialect. |
| Nikola | Short, excited questions. His "how?" leads into the explanation cards. |
| Faust | Unhurried, with machine sayings, and knows more than he says |
| First Automaton | ALL-CAPS punched tape: literal, polite, ominous. "HVALA." |
| Majstor Kristijan | Terse handwritten notes in the notebooks, occasionally sly |
| The apprentice | Silent |

## Environmental Storytelling

### Visual Storytelling

**Set Dressing:** Handles worn smooth from being turned three times every morning, and gaps where automatons stripped parts.

**Environmental Details:**
- Kristijan's chalk marks: small, sly symbols on the machines, which only make sense once he understands the machine.
- The First Automaton's notices: punched tape pinned to machines, on pale tape with a distinct shape, so colour isn't the only signal.

**Visual Symbolism:** The Book against the notebook, the straight line, and a pendulum in the clock tower for Huygens.

**Color and Lighting:** As in the GDD: a grey blueprint city, with brass and copper only on machines he has restarted.

---

### Audio Storytelling

**Ambient Design:** Silence at the start. Each restart adds a loop, as in the GDD.

**Music Integration:** The loops drop out briefly at room 7, for tension. At room 13 every loop plays together and the clock strikes.

**Voice Elements:** No voice acting.

**Sound Design Narrative:** Notices print with a teleprinter clatter. Kristijan's notes appear with the scratch of a pen.

---

**Document Types:**
- Kristijan's notebook notes, one per machine
- The First Automaton's notices, pinned to machines
- Torn logbook pages, carried by automatons

**Quantity:** About 13 notebook notes, 10 notices and 3 torn pages.

**Content Focus:**

**Discovery:**

- Optional: the 3 torn pages, carried in the armour 30, 45 and 60 lairs. Winning returns the page to his notebook.

**Rewards:** The torn pages are lore only. They give fights a story reward besides parts, open nothing, and come out of the existing line budget.

### In-Game Storytelling

**Primary Methods:**
- One or two lines in speech bubbles on the map after each restart
- Notices and notebook notes on the machines themselves
- The city's log, where every line can be re-read

**Show vs. Tell Balance:** Mostly shown, through the city's colour, motion and sound. Lines are short and never explain the maths; explanation cards do that.

**Interruption Approach:** Never during a room. Story arrives only between rooms.

**Player Control:** He taps to continue, and can re-read anything in the city's log.

**Opening:** Play first. The game opens on the stopped door lift, Penkala turning the handle three times, and one line. Once the door opens, someone finally asks who he is, and that's when he names his character and picks a look. Naming is a reward for proving himself, and no setup screen comes before play.

## Gameplay Integration

### Narrative-Gameplay Connection

**Integration Approach:** Story arrives between rooms and reacts to what he has restarted. It never explains the maths.

**Mechanic-Theme Alignment:**
- Writing lines is what moves the machines, so knowing why beats knowing the steps in his hands, not just in the story.
- Kristijan's planted mistake is the same move as his planted mistakes for friends.
- The automatons strip the city for parts, so it fits that they take his when he loses.
- A friend opening his link arrives as a new apprentice, so the one-minute intro is in the story too.

**Story-Gameplay Balance:** Almost all gameplay. Story is 60 short lines across the district.

**Ludonarrative Considerations:** A beaten automaton winds down instead of being destroyed. It drops parts and its punched tape, then rewinds and returns later. That explains why lairs restock, keeps Kristijan's helpers redeemable for a later district, and stays kid-friendly.

---

### Story Gating

**Gating Approach:** Story never gates anything. Only room order, as in the GDD, gates progress.

**Story-Locked Elements:** None.

**Cutscene Triggers:** None; there are no cutscenes.

**Mandatory Story Beats:** The lines at rooms 7 and 13, each a tap to continue.

**Optional Narrative:** The torn logbook pages and the fight notices.

---

### Player Agency

**Agency Level:** He watches the story unfold, with a few cosmetic choices.

**Player Influence:** His name, his look, and the order of rooms 5–6 and 9–10.

**Choice System:** None.

**Role-Playing Freedom:** His real creative say is in the rooms he builds, which hang in the workshop as his part of the story.
